# One Source, Two Retail Books — BHT Publication Engineering Record 001

**Status:** Completed comparative record from frozen Apple and Kindle EPUB 1 releases
**Date:** 2026-10-07
**Scope:** What should be remembered when one semantic publication is engineered for Apple/non-Kindle and Kindle reading systems in parallel
**Boundary:** This record explains the completed derivatives. It does not authorize mutation of either frozen release.

---

## 1. The central finding

BHT did not become two manuscripts.

It became two engineered packages whose structures differ because the reading systems apply different pressure to the same semantic work.

> The book has a stable semantic anatomy, but no single mandatory physical skeleton.

The source remains authoritative. Platform structure is allowed to differ only where it preserves a relation the reader needs.

## 5. Apple / non-Kindle track

### What Apple pressure revealed

The Apple/non-Kindle line primarily exposed:

- fragmentation blocked by containment;
- `overflow:hidden` becoming a pagination boundary;
- page width differing from outer application-window width;
- live graphics entering gutters or collapsing at two-page measures;
- old cosmetic page-break rules becoming obsolete after structural splitting;
- the need to preserve quiet space rather than automatically fill it.

### Final Apple structure

- 22 major semantic entry documents;
- no Kindle-only split documents;
- `shared.css`;
- `apple.css`;
- `cover.css`;
- direct-document major TOC;
- cover, title-page, copyright-page, and bodymatter landmarks;
- live CSS/HTML and SVG/CSS constructions retained where their relation survived;
- no Kindle plate directory;
- no Kindle decorative crack or closing-light assets.

### Why live graphics stayed live

Apple did not need the Kindle plate set merely because that set existed.

Where Apple preserved the relation, the live construction remained the better representation because it retained:

- responsive behavior;
- live text;
- inspectable structure;
- platform-native rendering;
- less package duplication.

## 6. Kindle track

### What Kindle pressure revealed

The Kindle line primarily exposed:

- fragment-dependent logical TOC destinations failing conversion;
- live CSS/SVG constructions keeping pieces while destroying spatial relation;
- pale text surviving while a distant dark field disappeared or repainted late;
- content duplication, ghosting, and delayed repaint around unstable boundaries;
- break controls failing where a real spine boundary was required;
- generated pseudo-element decoration failing as the sole carrier of a visual event.

### Final Kindle structure

- the same 22 major semantic entry documents;
- direct-document major TOC;
- `shared.css`;
- `kindle.css`;
- `cover.css`;
- two explicit renderer-stability continuation documents;
- 38 plate-family members;
- two explicit decorative assets;
- selected full-resolution admitted representations where live relation failed.

### Renderer-stability split 1: Jules

Boundary:

`Presence → Startup & Settle`

Evidence:

Repeated forward/backward traversal produced unstable repaint and duplicate/partial states until the movement crossed a hard content-document boundary.

Removal test:

Remove only after a future Kindle renderer demonstrates stable traversal of the same content without the split.

### Renderer-stability split 2: Western

Boundary:

`Present Recognition → Human authority and family → Human`

Evidence:

The dark banner fragmented, moved, or repainted late through softer repairs. A true spine/document split stabilized the passage.

Removal test:

Remove only after a future Kindle renderer demonstrates stable traversal without the split.

### Plate standing

Kindle plates were not selected because raster is inherently safer.

They were admitted where the renderer could preserve objects but not the relation among them.

The final pattern was:

- keep prose live;
- keep separable captions live;
- rasterize only the bounded spatial relation;
- retain explicit alt/caption relationships;
- review locally and in Kindle;
- admit exact bytes by human authorization;
- preserve whole-page review as a separate judgment.

### Decorative events

The Spot:

- generated glow/bloom proved fragile;
- final Kindle representation uses `spot-crack.png`;
- the live sentence remains live.

The closing edge:

- final Kindle representation uses `closing-edge-light.png`;
- it supports the page-turn gesture rather than replacing prose.

## 7. Human judgments that changed the engineering

### Blank space was not treated as waste

Several blank or nearly blank pages initially looked suspicious.

They were retained when they:

- gave a movement room;
- carried pacing;
- let a page turn perform meaning;
- did not hide missing or duplicated content;
- did not arise from unstable flow.

The test became:

> Is the quiet page doing compositional work, or is it evidence of a failure?

### Page turns could carry meaning

The separated final declaration:

> The next movement belongs to you.

was accepted because the reader physically performs the transition.

That acceptance would be lost by treating every page boundary as an efficiency problem.

### Blanket rasterization was rejected

Kindle failures did not turn every graphic into a plate.

Apple's successful live behavior and Kindle's surviving live cases both showed that representation should escalate only where relation fails.

### Blanket forced page breaks were rejected

Apple showed that containment and real page measure could be the cause.

Kindle showed that CSS break hints could be insufficient when the failure was repaint/state reconstruction.

The repair had to match the failure class.

### Apparent source defects could be renderer behavior

Ghost text, duplicate banners, disappearing backgrounds, and partial decorative states often resolved or changed during back-and-forth traversal.

That behavior pointed to renderer reconstruction rather than manuscript meaning.

### A technically neat solution could be compositionally wrong

Removing space, merging the final declaration backward, or normalizing Apple and Kindle into one skeleton would have simplified the package while weakening the reading.

Technical neatness was not allowed to outrank the book.

## 8. When the files became books

The Apple line crossed from repaired file to book when the semantic-entry rebuild could be read beginning to end without the package architecture calling attention to itself.

The Kindle line crossed that boundary when:

- conversion was stable;
- all graphic relations landed;
- the two repaint boundaries traversed smoothly;
- the Spot and closing gesture returned;
- the whole-book click-through felt faster, calmer, and properly paced.

The shared release criterion was not identical markup.

It was that each implementation disappeared behind the same reading.

## 10. Method for a future book

1. Establish the semantic source and exact identity.
2. Identify major semantic entries.
3. Build one entry document per major semantic entry by default.
4. Build direct-document major navigation.
5. Create shared CSS and narrow platform layers.
6. Register graphics by semantic relation, not by platform file.
7. Keep live representations where relation survives.
8. Escalate flow and graphic failures on separate branches.
9. Record every platform deviation with a reason class and removal test.
10. Prove content/source equivalence mechanically where possible.
11. Run standards validation.
12. Run platform-native conversion/import.
13. Review graphics in the actual platform.
14. Perform a whole-book human pass.
15. Freeze exact bytes.
16. Treat retailer ingestion as a separate crossing.

---

## 11. What not to carry forward

Do not carry forward:

- a successful retailer file as the new manuscript source;
- Kindle split documents as universal chapter architecture;
- Kindle plates as Apple defaults;
- Apple live graphics as proof that Kindle should keep them live;
- historical CSS workarounds after stronger structure makes them unnecessary;
- filenames as identity;
- human acceptance inferred from a clean validator;
- validator success inferred from a human click-through;
- blank-space removal as an automatic optimization.

---

## 12. Held conclusion

> One source can produce two legitimately different retail books.

The differences are valid only when they are:

- traceable;
- bounded;
- evidence-based;
- platform-specific;
- semantically faithful;
- reviewed in context;
- frozen by exact identity.

The aim is not to make the packages resemble each other.

The aim is to let both disappear behind the same reading.
